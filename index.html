Overview

Abstract

Low-latency generative speech enhancement must balance content reliability, acoustic quality, and latency. Temporal autoregression conditions on generated history, but under limited look-ahead a semantic-token error can affect later predictions more strongly than in offline models with full-utterance context. Fully factorized NAR prediction avoids this feedback but ignores ordered residual dependencies across RVQ codebooks. We ask whether selective autoregression can solve two coupled problems: semantic prediction without generated history and residual reconstruction that preserves RVQ order.

Accordingly, EDGE-GSE uses a sliding-window Qwen2-0.5B Transformer to predict a w2v-BERT 2.0-distilled CB1 without feedback from generated tokens; depth-AR reconstructs CB2–CB10 within each frame, while a residual-only GRU carries acoustic history. EDGE-GSE supports a minimum 26.67-ms configured input-context delay. On URGENT 2025, it better balances naturalness and ASR errors than several offline baselines and achieves state-of-the-art quality among compared low-latency streaming systems at their reported operating points. An on-device demo of EDGE-GSE running in real time on an MTK edge chipset, together with audio samples, is available on this page.

Architecture

Selective time/depth autoregression

Semantic prediction avoids generated-token feedback; acoustic reconstruction preserves the native coarse-to-fine RVQ order.

01

Time-NAR semantics

Qwen2-0.5B predicts CB1 without feeding earlier generated semantic tokens back into the semantic path.

02

Depth-AR acoustics

CB2–CB10 are generated in their native RVQ order within the same physical codec frame.

03

Residual-only history

An auxiliary GRU carries acoustic context across frames without reopening feedback into CB1.

04

Bounded look-ahead

Discrete operating points span 26.7–240 ms configured input-context delay, with 186.7 ms as the default.
