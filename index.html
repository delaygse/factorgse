FactorGSE: Selective Autoregression for Low-Latency Streaming Generative Speech Enhancement

Xin Yuan1,* Junling Lv1,* Xingjun Tan1 Rong Liu1 Zezhou Xu2 Yanqiang Lei1,†

1 Guangzhou Shiyuan Electronic Technology Company Limited, Guangzhou, China
2 Shanghai University of Finance and Economics, Shanghai, China

* Xin Yuan and Junling Lv contributed equally to this work. † Yanqiang Lei is the corresponding author.

Abstract

Autoregression need not be an all-or-nothing choice for codec-based streaming speech enhancement. Temporal feedback can propagate semantic errors, especially with limited look-ahead, whereas fully non-autoregressive (NAR) decoding omits explicit coarse-to-fine dependencies among residual vector quantization codebooks. We introduce FactorGSE, a selective factorization that uses time-non-autoregressive (time-NAR) prediction for a semantically distilled first codebook from bounded degraded speech without generated-token feedback, retains intra-frame depth-autoregressive (depth-AR) residual decoding, and uses residual-token recurrence to provide cross-frame acoustic context only for residual decoding. On 400 bilingual URGENT 2025 utterances, an aligned ablation shows 45.0% lower macro-averaged automatic speech recognition error without temporal feedback, while a double-blind listening test favors depth-AR over fully NAR. FactorGSE outperforms all evaluated streaming baselines in objective speech quality, recognition-based intelligibility, and speaker similarity. An edge implementation runs in real time on a 40-TOPS-class NPU at a 186.7-ms input-context budget. Audio samples and a real-time demo: https://delaygse.github.io/factorgse/.

Real-time streaming demo

The demo runs on an NPU-equipped edge device in a large meeting room (6 m long × 10 m wide) via a Zoom meeting. All Zoom audio processing, including noise suppression, is disabled; noise reduction and dereverberation come solely from FactorGSE.

English and Chinese scenarios are tested at speaker-to-microphone distances of 3, 6, and 10 m. Two participants appear on camera: one reads a fixed text from a script displayed on a phone while the other generates random noise.
